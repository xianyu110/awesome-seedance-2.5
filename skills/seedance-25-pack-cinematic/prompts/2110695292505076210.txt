how it was made, in four steps, with every prompt.

1. style sheet. before any shot, the film's van gogh: three key paintings, then ten panels from all over the film in the same manner. three colours only: blue for the world, yellow for warmth, red for the pep.

the key paintings (nano banana 2.1, from a grey blender plate + earlier paintings of the film):
THE SCENE: a 1970s concrete high school seen frontally across its courtyard, a big bare plane tree, a sweeper in a blue work jacket and an orange wool beanie sweeping red-orange leaves beside his green wheeled bin, students behind the lit classroom windows. Paint this scene as an oil painting in EXACTLY the manner of the attached style sheet and of the two attached paintings from the same film (the leaf in the swirling sky, the sketchbook over the courtyard): that manner is the one to keep, its colours, its deep cold blue, its swirling sky, its thick impasto, its warm lit windows, its red-orange leaves and beanie; do not invent another Van Gogh. Compared to those two paintings, push only two things: MORE BRUSHSTROKES EVERYWHERE, every surface made of visible loaded strokes laid side by side, no smooth area, the strokes following the forms (sky in swirls, walls in stacked horizontals, trunk in twists, ground in long horizontals, each leaf one thick dab); and THE PEOPLE MORE PAINTED: the figures built from a handful of bold strokes like Van Gogh's figures, their clothes dissolved into blended strokes of colour, no photographic face, no fabric detail, still recognisable by posture and colour (the sweeper: bent, blue jacket, orange beanie, broom; the students: dabs of warm colour behind the glass; the boy at the window: a few strokes, head turned to the glass). Same composition and placement as the scene; 21:9 canvas. The attached picture is not a photograph but the rendering of a crude architectural scale model with red handwritten notes saying what each shape must become: read the notes, make the real place out of it, then paint that real place as described; the notes disappear.

the panels (nano banana 2.1, each from its grey blender plate + the three key paintings + the sheet):
THE SCENE: [what each panel shows]. Paint this scene as an oil painting in EXACTLY the manner of the attached three paintings and the attached style sheet, all from the same film: keep that manner, its thick impasto, every surface made of visible loaded brushstrokes laid side by side, the strokes following the forms (sky in swirls, walls in stacked horizontals, trunks in twists, ground in long horizontals, each leaf one thick dab), its heavy contours, its deep cold blue dominating; its people painted in a handful of bold strokes, recognisable by posture and colour, never a photographic face. PALETTE OF THREE: the BLUE of the world (Prussian blue, cobalt, blue-grey, violet in the shadows), the YELLOW of warmth (the lit windows, the students' clothes, the lamplight, a chrome yellow that glows in the blue), and the RED for the pep (the red-orange leaves, the sweeper's beanie, the red smoke and flags, the painted nails). Same composition and placement as the attached grey plate, which is a crude architectural scale model of the scene: make the real scene from it, then paint it; if it carries red notes, read them and remove them. 21:9 canvas, no text.

2. blender. no prompt: the school is built as a grey world in python, then the frame of this shot from the classroom window, with a handheld camera move.

3. the painted still (nano banana 2.1, from the grey frame + the style sheet + the three key paintings):
THE SHOT (S2.01, beat 1, the landscape, the church at the back): Van Gogh, low horizon: from Matisse's window, the window post and the pier frame the courtyard, the wall, the roofs and the spire on axis; the sky takes more than half of the frame. Paint this scene as an oil painting in EXACTLY the manner of the attached style sheet and the three attached paintings from the same film: keep that manner, its thick impasto, every surface made of visible loaded brushstrokes laid side by side, the strokes following the forms (sky in swirls, walls in stacked horizontals, trunks in twists, ground in long horizontals, each leaf one thick dab), its heavy contours, its deep cold blue dominating; its people painted in a handful of bold strokes, recognisable by posture and colour, never a photographic face. PALETTE OF THREE: the BLUE of the world, the YELLOW of warmth (lit windows, lamplight), the RED for the pep (leaves, the sweeper's beanie, fire, red smoke and flags). The attached grey picture is a crude architectural scale model of the scene, rendered from the film's camera: keep EXACTLY its framing, lens, composition and the place, size and pose of every figure and object; make the real scene from it, then paint it. 21:9 canvas, cinematic, no text.

4. the animation (seedance 2.5, the still as first frame, the blender camera as motion reference, the style sheet as image reference, 8 seconds):
A moving oil painting in EXACTLY the manner of the attached style sheet (the film's Van Gogh), 24 fps, 21:9: every frame is thick paint, impasto, visible loaded brushstrokes, heavy contours; the strokes themselves breathe and swirl over time. Start EXACTLY on the start image: from the first-floor window of the classroom, a window post at the left, the courtyard wall, the roofs of the suburb, the church spire on the horizon, and a huge sky filling most of the frame. THE SKY CHANGES COLOUR LITTLE BY LITTLE, over the whole 8 seconds, never suddenly: it starts calm, all deep Prussian and cobalt blue with pale swirls; from the first seconds the swirls begin to turn faster and heavier; a glow of orange appears low on the horizon behind the roofs and the spire, then climbs into the swirls stroke by stroke, orange and red-orange paint mixing into the blue, the clouds thickening and darkening into storm clouds; by the end the upper sky is a churning storm of blue and orange, menacing, as if a fire or a war were rising beyond the town; a far rumble of thunder; a few leaves and birds carried by the wind. The town below stays still, a few windows glow yellow. THE CAMERA is a person standing at the window, holding it by hand: a very gentle, natural handheld breathing, a slow drift of a fraction of a degree, a faint tremor of the hands, as in the attached reference video (a layout previs on the empty set: take from it exactly this camera motion and its timing); no pan, no zoom, no jolt, no fast shake, never steady like a tripod. No text, no cuts, real time. Sound: wind, a distant rumble rising.
